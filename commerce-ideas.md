# Luma & Form — AI Commerce Prototype Brainstorm

## Three visual directions

### 1. Soft Lab Commerce
**Very Brief Intro:** Warm mineral surfaces, editorial product photography, and translucent analysis panels make online shopping feel like an intelligent studio consultation.
**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Electric Bazaar
**Very Brief Intro:** A high-energy dark marketplace with chromatic product cards and kinetic recommendation ribbons. The experience would feel playful, fast, and discovery-led.
**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Field Notes Storefront
**Very Brief Intro:** A botanical field journal aesthetic uses paper texture, specimen labels, and calm practical language to make product discovery feel deliberate and sensory.
**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach: Soft Lab Commerce

### Design Movement
Contemporary beauty-editorial design fused with a tactile digital lab notebook. The AI experience should feel like a personal retail consultant with clear reasoning, not a generic assistant bubble.

### Core Principles
1. Product discovery is led by needs, routines, and outcome rather than an overwhelming catalogue.
2. Every recommendation visibly explains why it fits the selected preferences.
3. Warm, tactile retail atmosphere sits beside precise product and routine information.
4. Browser-local demo data is disclosed in account and checkout settings without interrupting shopping flow.

### Color Philosophy
Milk-white and warm stone create a premium retail canvas. Oxide red identifies immediate commerce actions, forest green marks verified-fit recommendation signals, and deep ink carries editorial contrast. The palette feels human and material rather than overtly technical.

### Layout Paradigm
An editorial storefront uses broad, asymmetric feature bands that lead into a modular shopper workspace. Product cards appear in staggered shelves, while the AI consultation opens as a right-side companion panel that can be expanded without breaking the browsing path.

### Signature Elements
1. A rounded “fit rationale” label showing a product’s reason for recommendation.
2. Fine rust-red route lines linking quiz choices to recommendation cards.
3. Ingredient-specimen chips and material swatches that make product detail tactile.

### Interaction Philosophy
The shopper can take a short ritual quiz, refine a recommendation, save items, build a routine, add products to cart, select delivery, place a simulated order, and revisit local order history. AI-like messages are clearly labelled as a simulated local concierge.

### Animation
Product cards use subtle upward reveal and image scale on hover. The concierge drawer uses a swift side-slide, recommendation chips cascade after quiz selection, and cart feedback is immediate and tactile. Motion is under 240ms and respects reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use **DM Serif Display** for editorial product storytelling and **Manrope** for a clean, contemporary commerce UI. Prices and ingredient percentages use tabular figures.

### Brand Essence
**Luma & Form is a thoughtful self-care shop for people who want an adaptive routine, not more things.**

Personality: **considered, sensorial, candid**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are inviting and specific. CTAs speak to the ritual or decision at hand, never to generic conversion.

Example lines:
- “A better ritual starts with one good question.”
- “Keep what works. Let the rest wait.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A subtle monogram of two interlocking soft arcs representing light and form, paired with an editorial serif wordmark. The symbol is used alone as the favicon and account mark.

### Signature Brand Color
**Luma Oxide — #C95A40**

## Style Decisions

- Luma & Form is a separate portfolio case study from AtlasDesk and SyncPilot; it must carry its own wordmark, voice, retail palette, and product logic.
- AI-style concierge responses must be labelled as simulated local recommendations whenever the user opens the concierge or account settings.
- The store must demonstrate practical commerce interactions—catalogue filtering, product detail, wishlist, cart, checkout simulation, and local order history—rather than only a landing page.
