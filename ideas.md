# AtlasDesk — Design Brainstorm

## Teen visual approaches

### 1. Evidence Ledger
**Very Brief Intro:** Deep ink workspace with aurora-blue signal lines and precise evidence cards. The feeling is calm, reliable, and investigative rather than a flashy chatbot.
**Probability:** 0.06

### 2. Editorial Knowledge Room
**Very Brief Intro:** Warm paper, editorial typography, and index-card interactions make company knowledge feel like a curated research library. It would prioritize quiet reading and thoughtful review.
**Probability:** 0.03

### 3. Clinical Signal Grid
**Very Brief Intro:** Near-white surfaces, cobalt information bands, and structured data panels create a high-clarity operations console. It feels analytical, compact, and controlled.
**Probability:** 0.08

## Chosen approach: Evidence Ledger

### Design Movement
Dark editorial systems design combined with a restrained “mission control” interface. The product should feel like an evidence trail, not a generic AI chat app.

### Core Principles
1. Every answer is paired with visible evidence, confidence, and a human review state.
2. The shell remains quiet and dark while information-bearing cards create selective depth.
3. Attention is directed by a single cobalt-to-cyan signal language, never by noisy gradients.
4. Dense operational information stays legible through generous spacing, strong hierarchy, and short labels.

### Color Philosophy
The base is carbon ink and blue-black to suggest security and concentration. Electric cobalt marks active work, cyan marks verified evidence, and restrained amber marks review-needed items. The palette expresses confidence without pretending certainty.

### Layout Paradigm
A persistent left “evidence rail” anchors navigation. The centre carries the current operational task, while a context pane on the right exposes source quality, confidence, and activity. On narrow screens, this becomes a stacked workbench rather than a squeezed desktop dashboard.

### Signature Elements
1. A thin animated signal line that visually connects evidence cards to answers.
2. Confidence capsules with small evidence bars rather than generic status dots.
3. A “local demo” disclosure badge in the application shell so persistence is never misrepresented as a cloud backend.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should make inspection feel immediate: upload a demo document, ask a question, pin a source, mark a response helpful, and clear/reset local demo data. Local browser persistence powers only the demo state and is disclosed in-product.

### Animation
Cards enter with short vertical fades (40–60ms stagger, <220ms total). Signal lines pulse only after a response is generated. Buttons compress slightly on click, and nav changes slide gently without delaying keyboard interactions. Reduced-motion users see instant state changes.

### Typography System
Use **Space Grotesk** for display hierarchy and product identifiers, paired with **IBM Plex Sans** for UI and body copy. Answer text stays compact; numbers and source IDs use tabular alignment.

### Brand Essence
**AtlasDesk turns scattered team knowledge into reviewable, evidence-led support answers for operations teams.**

Personality: **assured, methodical, lucid**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and evidence-oriented; CTAs describe the immediate action rather than sell a vague benefit. Microcopy is calm and transparent.

Example lines:
- “Ask once. Trace every answer.”
- “Review the evidence before you send.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A compact atlas-pin mark formed by two offset cobalt arcs and a cyan centre point, paired with the AtlasDesk wordmark. The mark should work independently in the rail and favicon.

### Signature Brand Color
**Atlas Cobalt — #5B6CFF**

## Style Decisions

- AtlasDesk must use the AtlasDesk wordmark, carbon-ink workspace, Atlas Cobalt, cyan evidence accents, and Evidence Ledger voice on every AtlasDesk route.
- Generic glowing-AI imagery is not the product focal point. Answer provenance, source cards, confidence, citations, and review states are the visible product story.
- A cobalt-to-cyan signal line connects question, answer, source, and confidence states whenever AtlasDesk explains an evidence flow.
- SyncPilot is a deliberately separate portfolio case study with its own warm paper, deep-teal, coral design system. It is explicitly labelled as case study 02 so it is not presented as an AtlasDesk sub-route.
- Luma & Form is a deliberately separate portfolio case study 03 with an approved Soft Lab Commerce direction documented in `commerce-ideas.md`; it is not an AtlasDesk sub-route and must visibly carry its own brand label, Luma Oxide retail palette, and transparent browser-local commerce disclosure.
